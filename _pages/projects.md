<section class="research-project" id="proposal-authoring" aria-labelledby="proposal-project-title">
  <div class="project-topline"><span class="project-number">01 / RESEARCH PROJECT</span><span class="project-category">Industry-supported · AI for Services LLC</span></div>
  <h2 id="proposal-project-title">Trustworthy Neurosymbolic AI for Research Proposal Authoring</h2>
  <p class="project-summary">Develop an AI research assistant that helps researchers prepare NSF, NIH, and other research proposals—from interpreting a funding opportunity to organizing evidence, drafting sections, and checking requirements.</p>

  <div class="project-facts">
    <div><span>Industry partner</span><strong><a href="https://aiforservices.com/">AI for Services LLC</a></strong></div>
    <div><span>Research funding</span><strong>USD 21,975</strong></div>
    <div><span>Assigned researcher</span><strong><a href="https://github.com/chirix33">Ashraf Abdul-Muumin</a></strong><small>Roy research group</small></div>
    <div><span>Academic supervisor</span><strong>Dr. Kaushik Roy</strong><small>The University of Alabama</small></div>
    <div><span>Company contact</span><strong>Raj Krishnan</strong><small>Founder, AI for Services LLC</small></div>
  </div>

  <div class="project-content-grid">
    <div class="project-narrative">
      <h3>Research objective</h3>
      <p>Research proposals combine scientific arguments with sponsor-specific instructions, eligibility conditions, budget requirements, supporting documents, and submission rules. The project investigates how an AI assistant can represent these requirements explicitly and use them throughout authoring, instead of relying on a language model to remember and apply them from text alone.</p>

      <h3>Intellectual merit</h3>
      <ul class="project-contributions">
        <li><strong>Knowledge- and process-grounded authoring.</strong> Develop representations that connect solicitation requirements, document sections, supporting evidence, and dependencies between authoring tasks.</li>
        <li><strong>Neurosymbolic agent architectures.</strong> Study agents that combine language generation and information retrieval with explicit planning, constraint checking, and coordinated verification.</li>
        <li><strong>Reliable and auditable assistance.</strong> Investigate mechanisms for detecting missing evidence, inconsistent claims, and changing sponsor requirements while preserving traceable decisions and human review.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>

    <aside class="project-research-panel">
      <span class="project-panel-label">PROPOSED RESEARCH WORKFLOW</span>
      <ol class="project-process-list">
        <li><span>01</span><div><strong>Interpret</strong><small>Funding calls and authoring requirements</small></div></li>
        <li><span>02</span><div><strong>Represent</strong><small>Rules, evidence, and document structure</small></div></li>
        <li><span>03</span><div><strong>Author</strong><small>Coordinated research-writing agents</small></div></li>
        <li><span>04</span><div><strong>Verify</strong><small>Traceable checks and researcher review</small></div></li>
      </ol>
      <p class="project-panel-note">Research emphasis: accuracy, robustness to changing requirements, completeness, evidence traceability, and auditability.</p>
    </aside>

<section class="research-project" id="robot-control" aria-labelledby="robot-project-title">
  <div class="project-topline"><span class="project-number">02 / RESEARCH PROJECT</span><span class="project-category">Joint project · BRAINS Lab</span></div>
  <h2 id="robot-project-title">Neurosymbolic Methods for Robot Control</h2>
  <p class="project-summary">Develop robot-control methods that combine learned perception and action models with explicit task knowledge, physical constraints, and checks on what a robot can safely do.</p>

  <div class="project-facts">
    <div><span>Project status</span><strong>Joint research project</strong><small>Roy group + <a href="https://sites.ua.edu/brains/">BRAINS Lab</a></small></div>
    <div><span>Institution</span><strong>The University of Alabama</strong></div>
    <div><span>Co-supervisors</span><strong>Dr. Kaushik Roy</strong><small><a href="https://eng.ua.edu/eng-directory/dr-noorbakhsh-amiri-golilarz/">Dr. Noorbakhsh Amiri Golilarz</a></small></div>
    <div><span>Roy group</span><strong><a href="https://github.com/chirix33">Ashraf Abdul-Muumin</a></strong></div>
    <div><span>BRAINS Lab researchers</span><strong><a href="https://sites.ua.edu/brains/team/">Manish Kansana</a> · Mohammed</strong></div>
  </div>

  <div class="project-content-grid">
    <div class="project-narrative">
      <h3>Research objective</h3>
      <p>A robot must translate high-level goals into actions while accounting for changing observations, the capabilities of its hardware, and the consequences of each action. This joint project studies representations and control architectures that make those relationships explicit and usable during both planning and execution.</p>

      <h3>Intellectual merit</h3>
      <ul class="project-contributions">
        <li><strong>Executable representations of robot skills.</strong> Model actions through their inputs, preconditions, expected effects, physical constraints, and available control interfaces, connecting task-level reasoning to robot behavior.</li>
        <li><strong>Integrated perception, reasoning, and control.</strong> Investigate architectures that combine neural perception and learned policies with structured planning, state estimation, and runtime constraint checks.</li>
        <li><strong>Adaptation with verifiable behavior.</strong> Study how robots can recover from uncertain observations, failed actions, and changes in tasks or environments, while producing inspectable explanations of their decisions.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>

    <aside class="project-research-panel">
      <span class="project-panel-label">PROPOSED RESEARCH WORKFLOW</span>
      <ol class="project-process-list">
        <li><span>01</span><div><strong>Observe</strong><small>Sensors, objects, and task context</small></div></li>
        <li><span>02</span><div><strong>Represent</strong><small>Skills, constraints, and expected effects</small></div></li>
        <li><span>03</span><div><strong>Plan and act</strong><small>Grounded robot actions</small></div></li>
        <li><span>04</span><div><strong>Monitor and adapt</strong><small>Outcome checks and recovery</small></div></li>
      </ol>
      <p class="project-panel-note">Research emphasis: task success, safety, generalization, recovery from failures, and decision traceability.</p>
    </aside>
