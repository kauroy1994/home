My research asks a central question: **How can we build AI systems whose behavior can be grounded in knowledge and processes, inspected, and improved—rather than judged solely by predictive accuracy?** The work spans machine learning foundations and deployable AI systems, with rigorous evaluation in real applications.

## Current research directions

### Process-grounded reasoning

A document, procedure, or observed trajectory may encode more than descriptive facts: it can imply states, constraints, dependencies, and possible actions. We study ways to recover and use those structures, evaluate what can be verified, and expose uncertainty when the evidence is insufficient.

### Reliable, auditable AI agents

LLM-based agents can act on external systems, but plausible rationales are not sufficient evidence of correct execution. We explore architectures that separate proposed decisions from evidence checks, structural constraints, and independent verification.

### Neurosymbolic and causal learning

Our research combines learned representations with symbolic formalisms and causal understanding, aiming for models that generalize across changing settings while retaining inspectable decision logic.

### Healthcare and scientific applications

Healthcare process evaluation and diagnostic support motivate much of this work. For example, our [NeSy 2026 sepsis study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13617) combines semantic normalization with expert-defined fuzzy rules to assess protocol adherence; our [ACM healthcare article](https://doi.org/10.1145/3805697) examines LLM-assisted mental health assessment.
